## BDAY40 vs JUST21: the break-even is about two billing cycles

BDAY40 charges you 60% of the regular price for the first cycle only. JUST21 charges 79% of the regular price forever, renewals included. Because both discounts apply to the same base price, the comparison works out the same whatever your plan costs, in USD or CNY.

Using the CNY reference prices published with the anniversary promo for the entry 1 vCPU / 1 GB plan (regular CNY 32.65/mo, CNY 19.59 with BDAY40, CNY 25.79 with JUST21), on month-to-month billing over 12 cycles:

| Code | 12-cycle total |
| --- | --- |
| `BDAY40` | 19.59 + (11 × 32.65) = **CNY 378.74** |
| `JUST21` | 12 × 25.79 = **CNY 309.48** |

Month one goes to BDAY40 (CNY 19.59 vs 25.79). Month two flips it: CNY 52.24 versus CNY 51.58. From then on JUST21 pulls further ahead, and the 12-month gap lands around CNY 69.

The arithmetic: 0.60 + (n − 1) = 0.79n, so n ≈ 1.9 billing cycles. If you keep the server past the second renewal, JUST21 is the better code. Since almost nobody buys a VPS intending to cancel within 60 days, JUST21 is the default answer for a single server.

Annual billing changes the picture in a way worth understanding. BDAY40 discounts the first *payment cycle*, and on a 12-month term that cycle is the whole first year, so you'd pay 79% × 12 versus 60% × 12 for year one. JUST21 catches up at the same 1.9-cycle mark, which on annual billing means roughly two years. Two complications, though: long billing cycles on JustHost already carry their own discount (the site shows around 20% off for 12-month payment), the codes can't be combined with other discounts, and JustHost's own promo guidance is to compare actual cart totals because of exactly this. The 12-month column in the price table may therefore already be close to what a code gets you.

## Why your code didn't apply

JustHost's own instructions are three steps: pick a plan or build one in the configurator, enter one code at order time, confirm the discount has been applied before paying. Most failures come from one of these:

**Two codes in one order.** The terms say one promo code per order and no combining with other discounts. If you're eyeing BDAY40 for the first cycle and hoping to add something else, that won't work.

**The bundle codes on a custom build.** `3VPS2` and `5VPS3` are for preset Sirius bundles only, and the bundle FAQ says outright they don't apply to custom configurations. BDAY40 and JUST21 are the opposite: valid on all VPS plans *including* configurator builds.

**Assuming the discount is automatic.** It isn't. The code has to be entered during ordering, and the amount has to be visible on the order page before you submit payment.

One more thing people miss: the anniversary codes work for existing customers too, as long as the order is for a new service. Renewing or upgrading an existing server isn't a new order, so neither code applies there.

## 80 locations, shared ports, and what "unmetered" actually means

JustHost advertises coverage across 41 countries and around 80 data center locations, and the anniversary codes apply across the VPS locations taking part in the promo. The list includes Hong Kong, Tokyo, Singapore, Los Angeles, Seattle, New York, Chicago, Dallas, Atlanta, Toronto, Frankfurt, Amsterdam, London, Paris, Zurich, Helsinki, Stockholm, Warsaw, Milan, Palermo, Vienna, Prague, Riga, Vilnius, Bucharest, Sofia, Madrid and Dublin. Actual stock varies by location and is confirmed on the order page.

The port speeds in the table (100 Mbps up to 750 Mbps) describe a fair-share shared port, not dedicated bandwidth. JustHost's own material is explicit that the 300, 400, 500 and 750 Mbps figures don't represent dedicated bandwidth, and the theoretical monthly transfer figures quoted alongside them (93+ TB at 300 Mbps, 124+ TB at 400 Mbps, 155+ TB at 500 Mbps, 232+ TB at 750 Mbps) are calculated from port speed and explicitly not achievable continuously.

Practical translation: you won't be throttled for hitting a monthly cap, but you share the port, so sustained throughput depends on your neighbours. If you're near mainland China, test Hong Kong, Tokyo, Singapore and Los Angeles individually rather than picking by map distance. JustHost runs a Looking Glass tool so you can check latency before buying, which is worth ten minutes of your time given that you can change data center later from the control panel but migration disrupts a running server.

## Delivery, payment and refunds

JustHost accepts Alipay, PayPal and bank cards.

Delivery times come from user reviews rather than a published SLA. NVMe VPS orders are reported live anywhere from about 15 minutes to a couple of hours after payment, with 15–20 minutes being the common report. Dedicated servers are slower, around two business days in one reviewer's case. Support tickets get answered within about an hour according to the same reviews, and longer-term customers describe the KVM virtualisation as stable across repeat orders. Common complaints are a dated-looking control panel and a thin default OS template list.

JustHost references a 15-day money-back guarantee, but its own promo material qualifies that as subject to the current refund policy and the conditions of the specific plan. Check the terms on the plan you're buying rather than assuming 15 days applies universally.

On the value question, one user's comparison with DigitalOcean is fair: JustHost is cheaper for the specs, particularly with unmetered bandwidth, while DigitalOcean has the larger community and tutorial library. If you learn mainly by following third-party guides, that matters more than the price difference.
